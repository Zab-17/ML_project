\begin{abstract}
Egypt publishes no house-price index, and the pound lost about two thirds of its dollar value between 2021 and 2025. Asking prices moved faster than anyone's intuition. We specify a regression project that estimates the fair asking price of a residential unit listed for sale in Greater Cairo and is retrained as prices climb. We trace valuation from expert judgement to hedonic regression to tree ensembles, and say what each step fixed. Our own contribution is an area-quality index: one ordered number for how expensive a district is, built so it cannot leak the answer or go stale under inflation. A first pass leaves 15,786 usable listings.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{property valuation, hedonic pricing, regression, target encoding, concept drift, Egypt}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Motivation}
Urban inflation peaked at 38.0\% in September 2023~\cite{capmas_sep23} and still ran at 14.5\% in August 2026~\cite{capmas_aug26}. The exchange rate went from 15.6 pounds (EGP) per dollar in 2021 to 49.2 in 2025~\cite{wb_fx}. Savers moved into property: JLL figures in Ahram Online put 2024 sale-price growth at 180\% in New Cairo and 175\% in 6th of October~\cite{ahram2024jll}.

This is not a small corner of the economy. Construction and real estate produced 17.5\% of gross domestic product in 2024/25, roughly EGP~3.04 trillion~\cite{mped_gdp25}. Mortgage lending is still tiny at 0.3\% of GDP~\cite{dne2026mortgage} but grew 66\% in the first nine months of 2025~\cite{fra_mortgage}.

Yet nobody publishes a price. CAPMAS and the Central Bank issue no transaction index, and researchers note the absence of evidence-based price data in official sources~\cite{elkhishin2022}. What exists comes from portals, which admit their figures rest on sellers' own inputs~\cite{aqarmap2025trends}. Valuation is manual; the Financial Regulatory Authority added a model-valuation chapter in 2026 without saying what such a model must satisfy~\cite{enterprise2026valuers,dne2026fra}.

\subsection{Problem Specification}
\textbf{Task.} Predict the fair asking price, in EGP, of a residential unit listed for sale in Greater Cairo. This is supervised regression. We fit the logarithm of price per square metre and multiply back by area, so that villas do not dominate the squared error and districts stay comparable.

\textbf{Inputs.} Type, area, rooms, furnishing, completion status, payment method, the city--town--district--compound hierarchy, coordinates, amenities and the description.

\textbf{Output.} A point estimate with an interval, and a flag when the listed price sits far from it.

\textbf{Scope.} Cairo and Giza, including the new cities (New Cairo, Sheikh Zayed, 6th of October, Madinaty, Mostakbal City, Shorouk, the New Capital). Rentals, commercial units and coastal resorts are out; holiday demand follows different rules. The label is an asking price: no public Egyptian transaction data exists.

\textbf{Questions.} (1) Which model gives the lowest error? (2) How badly does a 2025-trained model do on 2026 listings? (3) What do location and text features add? (4) Does an ordered area-quality index beat one-hot district names where listings are few?

\section{Previous Work}

\textbf{Expert judgement.} A valuer inspects the unit and recalls comparable sales. Two valuers give two numbers and cannot explain the gap, and nobody revalues ten thousand mortgages this way.

\textbf{Hedonic regression.} Rosen argued that a buyer purchases a bundle of characteristics, each carrying an implicit price~\cite{rosen1974hedonic}; the sum is the property price and the estimator is a linear regression. Ames, 2,930 sales and 80 variables, became the teaching benchmark~\cite{decock2011ames}. The gain was auditability: a coefficient reads as the value of one more bedroom, which is why banks and statistical agencies still use them. The cost is the additive assumption. A bedroom is not worth the same in every district, and price does not rise in step with area.

\textbf{Tree ensembles.} Trees choose their own splits, so the interaction between district and size is learned rather than assumed. Ho et al.\ report test $R^2$ of 0.827, 0.903 and 0.904 for a support vector machine, random forest and gradient boosting on 39,554 Hong Kong transactions~\cite{ho2021predicting}. Tekin and Ucal Sari find mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) of 34.4\% for linear regression against 20.6\% for a random forest on 36,759 Istanbul listings~\cite{tekin2022istanbul}. Roughly, the line misses by a third of the price and the forest by a fifth. Machine learning also made valuation cheap to repeat, opened the door to coordinates and text, and allowed refitting as the market moves.

\textbf{Location and text.} Street and satellite imagery lifted $R^2$ from 81.7\% to 84.7\% on 40,470 London sales~\cite{law2019take}. That paper also holds a warning we took seriously: tested on a borough it had never seen, the same model scored between 62.7\% and 76.5\%. Advertisement wording helps too~\cite{abdallah2016text,baur2023automated}, and text with image embeddings cut error by 26\% on 52,851 Melbourne sales~\cite{hasan2024multimodal}.

\textbf{Moving markets.} Mayer et al.\ compared moving-window against extending-window refitting on 123,000 Swiss houses~\cite{mayer2019estimation}, and Tekin and Ucal Sari advise monthly retraining because Turkish prices outrun a quarter-old model~\cite{tekin2022istanbul}. Valuation became maintenance under concept drift~\cite{gama2014drift}. Balila and Shabri, meanwhile, had 247,517 Dubai transactions over 19 years, split them at random and reached $R^2$ 0.54~\cite{balila2024dubai}. Six times the data did not save a method blind to time.

\textbf{Egypt.} Mohamed et al.\ fitted a three-layer network to 1,990 units from 28 cities and report mean absolute error of EGP~85,460~\cite{mohamed2023egypt}, though their random split mixes eight inflationary years. Abdrabo et al.\ ran spatial hedonic regression in Alexandria~\cite{abdrabo2022alex}. El-Khishin and Rashwan surveyed 401 households and 421 developers because the official numbers were missing~\cite{elkhishin2022}. Alzain et al.\ priced Saudi properties from Aqar listings~\cite{alzain2022saudi}. We found no peer-reviewed study built on Egyptian portal listings. Table~\ref{tab:lit} collects the methods.

\textbf{Three traps.} Asking prices sit above sale prices~\cite{kolbe2021listings}, though list-price indices still track and lead sale trends~\cite{lyons2019list}; we name our target honestly. Duplicate advertisements are the central flaw of listing data~\cite{loberto2020} and must go before any split, or one property lands on both sides. Random cross-validation flatters spatial models~\cite{deppner2024spatial,roberts2017cv}, so our splits are time-ordered and district-grouped.

\section{Our Approach}

\textbf{Phase 2, cleaning.} Filter to residential sales in Cairo and Giza. Drop duplicates by listing identifier, then by a fuzzy key of type, rounded area, bedrooms, district, price and coordinates. Remove wanted-posts and per-district outliers in price per square metre, and parse size strings mixing feet and metres. Delete every personal field, including phone numbers buried in descriptions. Features: log area, rooms, completion status, payment method, amenity flags, listing age, distance to hubs, and TF--IDF keywords.

\textbf{The area-quality index.} A 150~m\textsuperscript{2} flat in Helwan and one in New Cairo differ mainly by address. One-hot district names capture that only by memorising each district: four listings teach nothing, an unseen district has no column at all, and 234 columns encode one idea. So we add, per district and per compound,
\[
q=\frac{\text{median price per m}^2\text{ of the area}}{\text{median price per m}^2\text{ across training listings}} .
\]
Three rules keep the index honest. It is built from training rows only and rebuilt inside every fold; computed once over everything, each test row would help price itself. It is stored as a ratio, not in pounds, because absolute prices inflate monthly while the standing of districts barely moves. And since the median district holds 4.5 listings, each district is shrunk towards its parent town by listing count~\cite{miccibarreca2001}, then binned into ordered tiers. We also keep a price-free description of location, distance to hubs and a compound flag, and will report accuracy with and without $q$.

\textbf{Phase 3, models.} Linear and ridge baselines, then K-nearest neighbours, a decision tree, random forest, gradient boosting and support vector regression.

\textbf{Phase 4, evaluation.} Mean absolute error in EGP, MAPE, root mean squared error on log price and $R^2$, under cross-validation grouped by district. The stress test trains on August 2025 and tests on March 2026 from the same portal, measuring seven months of drift; the two files share only 56 identifiers. A second 2026 file, shared rows removed, is an external test set.

\textbf{Use.} The model is meant for a web tool: enter a unit, get a range, a comparison against the district median and an over- or under-priced flag. Users report agreed sale prices, which become labels for retraining. Portal terms forbid scraping~\cite{pf_terms}, so nothing is scraped.

D1 is the working set: 15,786 of its sale rows fall in Cairo or Giza, all with coordinates and descriptions. Prices reach EGP~1 billion, furnishing is blank on half the sale rows, and thirteen columns hold agents' contact details. D2 gives the 2025 training period for the drift test; its price is text and the down payment is missing 73\% of the time. D3 overlaps D1 by 10,926 listings and keeps about 2,758 near-duplicates despite claiming none. D4 has no dates or identifiers, so it only shows the scale of the move: New Cairo sat near EGP~11,100 per square metre in 2022 against EGP~55,800 in D1. Three sets come from a portal that forbids automated collection~\cite{pf_terms}; we use them for coursework, strip personal fields and redistribute nothing.

\textbf{A first look.} Filtering D1 and trimming the extreme 1\% leaves 15,628 listings, median EGP~60,606 per square metre. The index runs from Mokattam at 1.65 through New Cairo at 1.07 and Madinaty at 0.95 down to Maadi at 0.57 and Badr City at 0.20. Two results changed our plan. Maadi ranks well below its reputation: its listings are older, larger resale flats (median 180~m\textsuperscript{2}, EGP~6.5m) while New Cairo sells new compound stock (190~m\textsuperscript{2}, EGP~11.4m), so price per square metre carries product type as well as address. And the portal is lopsided, New Cairo alone being 43\% of the rows while 171 districts hold fewer than twenty. What we build describes new-build Greater Cairo, and we will say so.
